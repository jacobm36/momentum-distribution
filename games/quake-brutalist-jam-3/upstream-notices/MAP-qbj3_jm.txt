*Author's Notes*

Autumn 2025, QBJ3 is announced. I've only made a handful of singleplayer maps at that point, so I was looking forward to joining more jams and community projects. Having made a bunch of ports of multiplayer maps between games, I already had a good grasp of how to use the editors at hand.

I am not a big fan of brutalist architecture, but I was still considering doing something for the jam. I looked up examples of brutalist buildings, and I realised that a lot of them look like the stuff I see whenever I go outside. Seeing how I have a fascination with recreating real-life locations in video games, I decided to take do this sort of thing here. I drove around my town(and neighbouring ones) to look for inspirations, and I settled on the area surrounding the Pope's statue in Sosnowiec.

Initially, I wanted to utilise OSM elevation data, but aligning the buildings to the slopes was a nightmare. Not to mention, I was running out of time. Thus, I had to limit the scope a bit. Despite that, the map still feels pretty gargantuan, despite its simple layout; I'm sorry about that. A few buildings were scrapped, and a bunch of textures became unused.

A lot of the assets were provided by me, since I took a lot of photos with my drone and my phone. I was curious whether I could use it for photogrammetry, so I recorded two flights around the statue just in case. Naturally, I used the built-in feature for taking spherical pictures to create the skybox; since my drone can't look directly upwards, I had to stitch it together with a HDRI off the Internet. After messing around with Meshroom, I managed to create a model of the Pope statue (which you can check out at https://skfb.ly/pDX7x ), and after learning about texture baking, I was able to optimise it for Quake.

As for the map itself, I suppose it's a snippet of an Eastern Bloc town, with its typical architecture. Despite Brutalism being associated with Soviet regimes, the level features a lot of contemporary elements, so I suppose it is a mixed bag of things from various time periods. The map has open progression, so have fun.
