Title                   : The Oppressing Facade

Description             : When QBJ3 got teased a long time ago I did some research on how my map could look like. I found this
			  amazing picture by German and Berlin-based painter Stefan Hoehnerloh (https://www.hoenerloh.de/de/galerie/galerie06.html) which
			  captures an eerie dreamlike concrete atmosphere which perfectly fits Quake's vibe, and also reminds me of my city of choice, Berlin.
			  I started using Makkon trimsheets and lots of nested linked groups to create a looming façade, separated some elements to create
			  variety so it didn't look as repetitive. Besides that I built corridor elements also with linked groups (pieces with 1/2/3
			  connections, straights and corners) so I could in theory easily stick together a layout quickly. This all was just the art test
			  phase, there was no gameplay, not even a general layout yet.

			  The layout planning started only after QBJ3 officially got announced and we got the progs. You can still see the sketch_layout layer
			  in the .map which I used for very high level planning. As for the high level gameplay I didn't want a overarching theme as in many
			  of my other maps, but rather embrace unique traps to surprise players. It was freeing and fun to think about little things to keep
			  players on their toes.

			  In terms of structure I wanted to have a linear introduction with a loop at the end. From there players reach the elevator and the
			  next "tier" of challenges: they could tackle one of two encounters in the order they preferred, both yielding a silver key, and two
			  silver doors which hold another (more difficult?) encounter in which each silver key gets "exchanged" if you will to a golden key.
			  Both golden keys are needed to reach the final encounter. This allows for more freedom, but with the tiered approach still doesn't
			  overwhelm players.

			  One difficulty working with this art style was that it looked "too real" in some ways. That and the fact I built all these bespoke
			  corridor pieces made encounter design not as flexible. In my previous maps or Quake general the locations are very abstract (mostly
			  generic factories with weird proportions, or something non-descriptive as my antigravity jam map). But this time the façade and the
			  corridors look like socialist architecture which people recognise, so I felt if the proportions are extreme (like the super high
			  ceilings in the cone spotlight rooms) it's fine, but if they are only halfway too off (I experimented with ceiling heights), it
			  looks weird. But of course it also can't be too realistic, because then everything would look too crammed for Quake's fast gameplay.
			  That's why I added some destruction to allow for higher ceilings, wider passages etc. Working with linked groups early made at least
			  iteration speed quick.

			  Thanks to the deadline extensions I could reach all my goals, even though I almost scrapped the water section. It also allowed me to
			  create these propaganda posters based on QBJ3's fantastic enemy roster to add a bit of environmental storytelling. Also the last
			  encounter took quite some time, as I wanted it to look special and thus couldn't rely on too much linked group recycling.
			  Oh and thanks to Rabbit I added a python script in my compile routine to offset the windows randomly in all the linked groups so
			  they didn't look repetitive.
